# Messaging Pillars

Five pillars. Each is a complete argument: the pain it names, the promise, the feature that proves it, and a CTA. Use these as the backbone for landing sections, ads, and posts — the language should stay consistent across surfaces.

---

## A. Stop wasting nights

- **Pain:** You end up home, scrolling, on nights you genuinely wanted to be out.
- **Promise:** Orbit gives you one concrete action for tonight — not a feed of 200 options.
- **Landing headline:** *You wanted to go out. Orbit tells you where.*
- **Paragraph:** The problem was never a shortage of events. It's the gap between 8pm intentions and actually being somewhere. Orbit's Solo Night Generator gives you three plans sized to your energy and budget — a minimum-viable one that still counts as a rep, up to a bold one — so the decision is made before the resistance wins.
- **Feature proof:** Solo Night Generator (three tiers, hard-filtered to your avoided settings).
- **CTA:** *Try the Solo Night Generator.*

---

## B. Weak ties become relationships through follow-up

- **Pain:** You meet people you click with, then let the connection quietly die.
- **Promise:** Orbit tracks who to follow up with and tells you what to say.
- **Landing headline:** *The person you met last week won't follow up for you.*
- **Paragraph:** Adult friendship is mostly logistics: remembering, reaching out, and reaching out again. Orbit keeps a private tracker of who you met and where, warns you when a tie is going cold, drafts the message in your voice, and hands it off to your own WhatsApp, SMS, or email. It never sends anything for you — it just removes every excuse not to.
- **Feature proof:** People tracker + follow-up generator with why-it-works, risk level, and conditional handoff buttons.
- **CTA:** *See how a follow-up draft works.*

---

## C. Social life needs reps, not vibes

- **Pain:** You keep waiting to *feel* social before acting social.
- **Promise:** Orbit treats social rebuilding like training mileage — the feeling follows the rep.
- **Landing headline:** *Your social life needs reps, not motivation.*
- **Paragraph:** Nobody waits to feel like running before they train for a race. Social life is the same and we pretend it isn't. Orbit logs the small reps — left the apartment, talked to one person, sent one follow-up — and shows you the streak and the repeat-exposure count. It reads like a training log, not a game. No badges, no confetti.
- **Feature proof:** Social rep tracker (16 rep types, weekly progress, streak, repeat exposure).
- **CTA:** *Start your 7-day plan.*

---

## D. Recurring exposure beats novelty

- **Pain:** You keep trying random new events but never become *known* anywhere.
- **Promise:** Orbit pushes you toward recurring anchors, because being seen three times is where friendship starts.
- **Landing headline:** *Become a regular somewhere.*
- **Paragraph:** The run club you attend once is a nice evening. The run club you attend for a month is a social life. Orbit's event scoring weights recurring events heavily and nudges you back to the same rooms, because repeated low-pressure exposure — not a constant search for something new — is how acquaintances turn into friends.
- **Feature proof:** Recurring-weighted event scoring + weekly review's "repeat this" recommendation.
- **CTA:** *Score your events.*

---

## E. Private by default

- **Pain:** Social apps feel performative, and a tracker of your relationships feels like something you'd never want public.
- **Promise:** Orbit is a private operating system, not a feed. Your data stays on your device.
- **Landing headline:** *A social life you don't have to perform.*
- **Paragraph:** Orbit has no feed, no profiles, no followers, and no share-your-streak mechanics. In demo mode everything lives in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere. It never reads your messages, never auto-sends, and never imports your contacts. It's the one place your social intentions can be honest because no one else is watching.
- **Feature proof:** localStorage-only demo mode, plain-English privacy note, export/delete controls, handoff-not-automation architecture.
- **CTA:** *Try the private demo — no account.*
