# Target Personas

Eight personas. Each includes pains, the emotional trigger, what they currently try, why it fails, Orbit's specific promise, the best acquisition channel, a landing angle, pricing sensitivity, best CTA, and example ad + LinkedIn + SEO copy.

**Ordering note:** lead with #1 and #2 in early marketing — they have the clearest pain and the least stigma about "rebuilding." Tune this order after the founder's dogfood week.

---

## 1. Post-divorce professional

- **Pains:** The married-couple social circle evaporated; weekends are suddenly empty; starting over feels humiliating at 40+.
- **Emotional trigger:** "My whole social life was routed through my marriage, and now it's gone."
- **Currently trying:** Occasional Meetup events, forcing themselves to text old friends, telling themselves they'll "figure it out."
- **Why it fails:** No system, so effort is sporadic and self-conscious. One bad night ends the whole attempt.
- **Orbit promise:** A private, non-humiliating way to rebuild rhythm one outing and one follow-up at a time.
- **Best channel:** Divorce-recovery communities, SEO ("how to make friends after divorce"), LinkedIn.
- **Landing angle:** *Rebuild your social rhythm one outing and follow-up at a time.*
- **Pricing sensitivity:** Low-to-medium — high willingness to invest in a genuine rebuild, sensitive to anything that feels like a dating upsell.
- **Best CTA:** *Start your 30-day rebuild.*
- **Ad copy:** "The couple-friends are gone. That's not the end of your social life — it's the start of one that's actually yours. Rebuild it in 30 days."
- **LinkedIn post:** "Divorce quietly deletes your social operating system. Not the friendships exactly — the logistics that kept them running. I've been rebuilding mine deliberately: one recurring event, one follow-up a week. Here's the system."
- **SEO title:** *How to Make Friends After Divorce Without Using Another Social App.*

## 2. New-city transplant

- **Pains:** Knows no one; the city feels like a lobby they're passing through; loneliness despite being surrounded by people.
- **Emotional trigger:** "I've lived here eight months and I still don't have anyone to call."
- **Currently trying:** Bumble BFF, Meetup, hoping work turns into friends.
- **Why it fails:** Matching apps produce one-off coffees that go nowhere; no mechanism for the repeat exposure that builds real ties.
- **Orbit promise:** Turn a new city into a real routine — recurring anchors and a system for following up.
- **Best channel:** Local subreddits, city-specific SEO, Instagram neighborhood accounts.
- **Landing angle:** *Turn a new city into a real social routine.*
- **Pricing sensitivity:** Medium.
- **Best CTA:** *Find your recurring anchors.*
- **Ad copy:** "New city, zero friends, eight months in. The fix isn't more first-meetings. It's becoming a regular somewhere. Orbit shows you where and keeps you going back."
- **LinkedIn post:** "Moving to a new city is a logistics problem disguised as a loneliness problem. You don't need to 'put yourself out there' more. You need two recurring events and a follow-up habit."
- **SEO title:** *How to Make Friends in a New City When You Work Remotely.*

## 3. Remote / hybrid worker

- **Pains:** The office was the social life; now the calendar is empty of humans; days pass without a real conversation.
- **Emotional trigger:** "I haven't talked to anyone but my laptop in three days."
- **Currently trying:** Coworking spaces, telling themselves Slack counts.
- **Why it fails:** No structural reason to leave the apartment; social contact requires deliberate design they haven't done.
- **Orbit promise:** Rebuild the social reps that remote work removed — starting with just leaving the apartment.
- **Best channel:** Remote-work communities, indie-hacker/build-in-public, X/Twitter.
- **Landing angle:** *Remote work deleted your social life. Rebuild it on purpose.*
- **Pricing sensitivity:** Medium — comfortable paying for tools.
- **Best CTA:** *Start your 7-day plan.*
- **Ad copy:** "Remote work is great until you realize the office was your entire social life. Orbit rebuilds the reps, starting with one: leave the apartment today."
- **LinkedIn post:** "Remote work quietly outsourced our social lives to an office that no longer exists. If you don't design social contact deliberately now, it doesn't happen. Here's how I designed mine."
- **SEO title:** *Social Life After Remote Work: A Rebuild Plan.*

## 4. Introvert who wants more friends

- **Pains:** Wants connection but finds open-ended socializing exhausting and awkward; defaults to solo.
- **Emotional trigger:** "I want more friends, I just dread the part where I have to get them."
- **Currently trying:** Occasionally forcing themselves out, then retreating for weeks.
- **Why it fails:** No pacing, no small on-ramps — every attempt is all-or-nothing and ends in overwhelm.
- **Orbit promise:** Small, structured reps sized to your energy, with an exit rule so you're never trapped.
- **Best channel:** Introvert communities, SEO, Reddit (carefully).
- **Landing angle:** *A private social plan for introverts who want to get out more.*
- **Pricing sensitivity:** Medium.
- **Best CTA:** *Try the Solo Night Generator.*
- **Ad copy:** "You don't need to become an extrovert. You need small reps, an exit rule, and one conversation at a time. That's the whole plan."
- **LinkedIn post:** "Introvert advice is usually 'just put yourself out there.' Useless. What works: structured events with a clear end time, a single micro-goal, and permission to leave after 45 minutes. Structure beats willpower."
- **SEO title:** *A 30-Day Social Rebuild Plan for Introverts.*

## 5. Faith / community-centered adult

- **Pains:** Wants their social life anchored in their faith or cultural community but struggles to show up consistently or go deeper than a nod.
- **Emotional trigger:** "I go to the mosque/church every week and still don't really know anyone there."
- **Currently trying:** Attending services, hoping familiarity turns into friendship on its own.
- **Why it fails:** Attendance without follow-up stays surface-level; no bridge from "familiar face" to "friend."
- **Orbit promise:** Turn the community you already attend into real relationships, respectfully and consistently.
- **Best channel:** Community word-of-mouth, faith-oriented newsletters, culturally-specific groups.
- **Landing angle:** *Become a regular somewhere that already matters to you.*
- **Pricing sensitivity:** Low-to-medium.
- **Best CTA:** *Turn familiar faces into friends.*
- **Ad copy:** "You show up every week and still only know names, not people. Orbit helps you follow up, remember, and go deeper — in the community you already belong to."
- **LinkedIn post:** "The highest-return social setting is usually one you already attend and under-use. For a lot of people that's their faith community. Consistency plus one follow-up a week changes it completely."
- **SEO title:** *How to Build Real Friendships in Your Faith Community.*

## 6. Fitness / social-routine builder

- **Pains:** Has a fitness habit but it's solitary; wants the run club / gym to double as a social anchor.
- **Emotional trigger:** "I see the same people at the 6am class every week and we've never spoken."
- **Currently trying:** Showing up, earbuds in, leaving immediately.
- **Why it fails:** No prompt to convert proximity into conversation; the routine stays parallel-play.
- **Orbit promise:** Use recurring fitness settings as friendship infrastructure — familiar faces into real connections.
- **Best channel:** Run clubs, fitness communities, Strava-adjacent audiences, Instagram.
- **Landing angle:** *Use recurring events to turn familiar faces into real connections.*
- **Pricing sensitivity:** Medium.
- **Best CTA:** *Score your recurring events.*
- **Ad copy:** "Same run club, same faces, zero friends. The proximity is already there. Orbit gives you the one nudge that turns it into a conversation, then a plan."
- **LinkedIn post:** "The best friendship infrastructure most people already have and waste: a recurring workout. You're seeing the same 15 people weekly. That's not a class, that's a pre-built social circle waiting for one hello."
- **SEO title:** *How to Make Friends at Your Run Club (Without Being Weird About It).*

## 7. High-performing professional with a weak social life

- **Pains:** Career is excellent, personal life is thin; knows the imbalance but treats socializing as unoptimizable.
- **Emotional trigger:** "I can run a team but I can't seem to build a weekend."
- **Currently trying:** Nothing systematic — assumes friends will materialize the way career wins did.
- **Why it fails:** Applies zero of their professional rigor to social life; leaves it to chance and loses.
- **Orbit promise:** Bring the same system-thinking you use at work to the social life you've been neglecting.
- **Best channel:** LinkedIn, professional networks, indie-hacker/build-in-public.
- **Landing angle:** *You systematize everything else. Systematize this.*
- **Pricing sensitivity:** Low — will pay for anything that works and saves time.
- **Best CTA:** *Start your 7-day plan.*
- **Ad copy:** "You'd never leave your pipeline to chance. Why your social life? Orbit gives it a system: weekly outings, follow-ups, a 30-day rebuild. Same rigor, different domain."
- **LinkedIn post:** "High performers apply systems to everything except the thing that most predicts long-term wellbeing: their relationships. I built a CRM for my social life for exactly this reason."
- **SEO title:** *Why Successful People Have Weak Social Lives — and the System That Fixes It.*

## 8. Single adult seeking dating/marriage momentum (without a dating app as the core)

- **Pains:** Wants a relationship but is burned out on swiping; senses that a real social life is the better path to meeting someone.
- **Emotional trigger:** "I don't want another dating app. I want a life where I'd actually meet someone."
- **Currently trying:** Dating apps, reluctantly.
- **Why it fails:** Apps optimize for volume of strangers, not the recurring real-world exposure where compatible people actually meet.
- **Orbit promise:** Build the social cadence where meeting someone becomes a natural byproduct — not the swipe grind.
- **Best channel:** SEO ("meet someone without dating apps"), Instagram, dating-burnout communities. **Handle carefully — never overpromise romance.**
- **Landing angle:** *Build a life where meeting someone is a byproduct, not a second job.*
- **Pricing sensitivity:** Medium.
- **Best CTA:** *Start building your cadence.*
- **Ad copy:** "Dating-app burnout is real. The alternative isn't 'try harder' — it's building a social life full enough that you meet people the old way. Orbit builds that." *(Never: "find love" / "meet your match.")*
- **LinkedIn post:** "The most underrated dating strategy is having a real social life. Recurring events, weak ties, actual community — the pool of people you meet naturally beats any algorithm. Build the life, not the profile."
- **SEO title:** *How to Meet People Without Dating Apps.*

**Claim caution for #8:** this persona is the closest to the "dating app" and "guaranteed outcome" lines. Copy must sell the *social life*, never the romantic result. No "find love," no "meet your person," no implied match.
