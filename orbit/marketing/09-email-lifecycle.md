# Email Capture & Lifecycle

## Capture

A waitlist / email-capture placeholder lives on the landing page and pricing page. In v0.1 it's a placeholder (no backend) — wire it to a real provider (Buttondown, ConvertKit, Resend) in Phase 2. Capture email only; never require more to try the demo.

## The five lifecycle emails

Each: short and direct, one practical action, one CTA back to Orbit. Voice per `14-brand-voice.md`. No guaranteed-outcome language anywhere.

### Email 1 — Welcome
**Subject:** You don't need more events. You need a system.
> You signed up because something about "stop starting from zero every weekend" landed. Here's the whole idea: the problem was never a shortage of things to do. It's the gap between wanting to go and actually being somewhere — and then following up afterward so it wasn't a one-off.
>
> **One action today:** open the demo and generate a plan for tonight. Even the minimum version counts.
>
> [Try the Solo Night Generator →]

### Email 2 — Your first social rep
**Subject:** Your first rep is smaller than you think.
> People think rebuilding a social life means a big night out. It doesn't. It means leaving the apartment once, on purpose, and staying long enough that something could happen.
>
> That's a rep. It's the whole assignment. The feeling of wanting to be social comes *after* the reps, not before — same as the gym.
>
> **One action today:** log one rep. Left the apartment counts.
>
> [Open your rep tracker →]

### Email 3 — Weak ties die without follow-up
**Subject:** The person you met last week won't follow up for you.
> Here's where almost everyone loses the thread: you meet someone good, you both say "we should hang out," and then nobody does. The tie dies quietly, not from a lack of interest but from a lack of logistics.
>
> The fix is one message, sent within a couple of days, that carries no pressure.
>
> **One action today:** pick one person you met recently and let Orbit draft the follow-up. You send it from your own phone — Orbit never sends anything for you.
>
> [Draft a follow-up →]

### Email 4 — Recurring events beat random events
**Subject:** Stop chasing novelty. Become a regular somewhere.
> You could go to a different event every week for a year and still not have a friend. Or you could go to the *same* event three times and start becoming a familiar face — which is where friendship actually begins.
>
> Repeated low-pressure exposure is the mechanism. Novelty is the trap.
>
> **One action today:** pick one recurring event and commit to going twice.
>
> [Score your events →]

### Email 5 — Upgrade prompt
**Subject:** Keep the 30-day rebuild going.
> Week one is motion. Weeks two through four are where weak ties turn into people you actually know — conversations, follow-ups, cadence. That's the part worth not stopping short of.
>
> Pro keeps the weekly plan coming, unlocks the full 30-day rebuild, and drafts follow-ups for everyone in your orbit.
>
> [See what's in Pro →]

## Sequence timing

- Email 1: immediately on signup.
- Email 2: day 2.
- Email 3: day 4.
- Email 4: day 7.
- Email 5: after the 7-day plan completes (event-triggered, not time-triggered, once analytics exist).

Never send more than one email in 48 hours. If someone's actively using the app, suppress the nudges — the product is the better message.
