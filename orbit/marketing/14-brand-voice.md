# Brand Voice

## Orbit sounds like

- Direct
- Calm
- Adult
- Practical
- Slightly contrarian
- Private
- Behavior-focused
- Encouraging without being soft
- Honest without being cruel

## Orbit never sounds like

- Cute
- Cheesy
- Clinical
- Corporate
- Pickup-artist
- Therapy-coded
- Overly masculine
- Overly spiritual
- Shame-heavy
- Bro-science

## The test

Read it aloud. If it sounds like a hype coach, a self-help book, a wellness brand, or a startup landing page from 2021, rewrite it. It should sound like a sharp, honest friend who has your number and isn't going to flatter you.

## Swaps (do this, not that)

| Instead of | Use |
|-----------|-----|
| "Find your tribe!" | "Become a regular somewhere." |
| "Make besties fast!" | "Turn weak ties into real relationships." |
| "Never feel lonely again!" | "Build a weekly rhythm that gives connection a chance." |
| "Put yourself out there!" | "Do one small rep today." |
| "You've got this! 💪" | "The dread before is worse than the thing itself." |
| "Unlock your best social life!" | "Stop starting from zero every weekend." |
| "Join our community!" | "A private system. No feed, no performing." |

## On directness vs. cruelty

Orbit's "avoidance mirror" names patterns bluntly — "You added four events and committed to none." That's the voice working. The line it never crosses: making someone feel pathetic, broken, or behind. Every direct observation is paired with one small, doable next rep. Name the pattern, hand over the rep, move on. Never diagnose, never shame, never pile on.

## On the guardrails as voice

The claim guardrails aren't just legal hygiene — they're the voice. A brand that refuses to promise you friends or love *because it respects you enough not to lie* sounds more trustworthy than every competitor that does. Lean into the honesty. "We can't promise you friends. We can promise you'll leave the apartment, talk to someone, and follow up — and that's what actually works" is on-brand and true.
