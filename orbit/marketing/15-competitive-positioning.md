# Competitive Positioning

**Core differentiation:** Orbit does not help you discover infinite options. Orbit helps you choose, attend, follow up, and repeat. Every competitor below is either a discovery tool or a storage tool. Orbit is an action tool.

| Competitor | What they do well | Why they don't solve the problem | How Orbit differs |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------------------------|-------------------|
| **Meetup** | Real inventory of recurring groups; genuine communities exist there | Discovery only — it lists events, then leaves you alone with the hard parts (going, talking, following up). Overwhelm, no follow-through. | Orbit scores what to attend, prepares you to go, and tracks the follow-up. It's the layer on top of Meetup, not a replacement for its inventory. |
| **Eventbrite** | Enormous event catalog; ticketing | Pure discovery + transactions; zero relationship layer; treats you as a buyer, not a person building a life. | Orbit is about repetition and relationships, not one-off ticket purchases. Recurring beats novelty. |
| **Bumble BFF** | Lowers the barrier to a first friend-match | Matching produces one-off coffees with strangers and no mechanism for the repeat exposure that builds real ties; feels like dating, performs like dating. | Orbit works with the people and events already in your life — no matching, no strangers-by-algorithm, no performing. |
| **Generic habit trackers** | Good at streaks and reminders | Domain-blind — they'll track "be social" like "drink water," with none of the social-specific structure (openers, follow-ups, reality checks). | Orbit's reps, reviews, and drafts are built for the specific mechanics of social rebuilding. |
| **Generic CRMs (Notion, Airtable, HubSpot-lite)** | Flexible storage of contacts | They're databases. People don't pay to maintain a database, and a CRM never tells you *what to say* or *whether to stop*. | Orbit answers the human questions — should I follow up, what do I say, am I overinvesting, should I let this go — not just "store this contact." |
| **Notes apps** | Frictionless capture | No structure, no prompts, no cadence — a note about someone you met is where the tie dies, not where it grows. | Orbit turns the capture into a tracked, prompted follow-up loop. |
| **Calendar apps** | Reliable scheduling | They hold events you already decided on; no scoring, no preparation, no social layer. | Orbit decides *what's worth attending* and prepares you for it, then hands off to your calendar (.ics) for the scheduling it's good at. |
| **AI companions (Replika, character chat)** | Always-available "connection" | They substitute for real relationships instead of building them — the opposite of the goal; they keep you in the apartment. | Orbit never talks *to* you as a friend. It pushes you toward real people and gets out of the way. It is explicitly not a companion. |
| **Dating apps** | Volume of romantic candidates | Optimize for swiping, not for a real social life; burnout-inducing; wrong tool for friendship and community. | Orbit builds the social life where meeting people (romantically or not) becomes a byproduct. Never a swipe, never a match. |

## The one-liner for a crowded slide

> Meetup finds the event. Your calendar holds it. A CRM stores who you met. Orbit is the only one that makes you go, talk to someone, and follow up — the parts that actually build a social life.

## Positioning against the whole category

The competitive frame isn't "a better Meetup" or "a friendlier CRM." It's: *everyone else optimizes discovery or storage; nobody optimizes follow-through.* Follow-through is the entire problem. Own it.
