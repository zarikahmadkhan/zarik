# Product-Led Growth Hooks

Shareable outputs that spread Orbit **without ever violating privacy.** The bright line: nothing that reveals real relationship data, private notes, or contacts can be shared. Only generic, non-personal artifacts.

## Safe to share (no personal relationship data)

- **A solo-night plan** — "here's a plan for a restless Tuesday" with no personal CRM data attached.
- **A 7-day social rebuild checklist** — the generic structure, not your specific events.
- **A generic follow-up script** — the template, not a message to a real named person.
- **A city guide** — Jersey City / NYC recurring-anchor recommendations.
- **A social-rep score summary** — "12 reps this month" with no names or details.
- **A printable 30-day rebuild calendar** — the program structure as a shareable artifact.

## Never shareable

- People CRM data
- Private notes
- Relationship reality checks (they name real people)
- Personal follow-up history
- Contact info
- Sensitive user goals — unless the user explicitly and specifically chooses to share a single generic item

## How each hook drives growth

- **Solo-night plan share:** a friend sees a genuinely useful plan → "what generated this?" → demo. Low-friction, high-utility.
- **30-day calendar:** printable/screenshot-able, lives on a fridge or desktop, ambient reminder + conversation starter.
- **Rep-score summary:** the "training log" framing is inherently shareable in fitness-adjacent circles without exposing anything private.
- **City guide:** SEO + social double-duty; useful to locals, drives Cluster 4 traffic.

## Design principle

Every shareable artifact must be useful to the *recipient* even if they never sign up (same bar as the SEO guardrails). A share that's just an ad doesn't spread; a genuinely useful solo-night plan does. And every shared artifact is generic by construction — the privacy promise is never the thing traded for growth.
