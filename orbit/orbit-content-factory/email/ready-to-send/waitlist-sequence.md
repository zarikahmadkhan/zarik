# Waitlist Sequence — 7 emails

Trigger: waitlist signup. Cadence: days 0, 2, 4, 7, 10, 14, 18. Suppress any email if the person has already entered the app that week — the product is the better message.

## Email 1 · Day 0 — Welcome
**Subject:** You don't need more events. You need a system.
**Preview:** The whole idea in 90 seconds, plus your first move.
**Segmentation:** everyone.

You signed up because something about "stop starting from zero every weekend" landed. Here's the entire idea:

Your social life doesn't have a discovery problem — you can find forty things to do this weekend in three taps. It has a follow-through problem: going, talking to one person, and following up so it wasn't a one-off. That loop, run weekly, is the whole system. Orbit exists to run it.

**One action today:** open the demo and generate a plan for tonight. The easy option counts.

## Email 2 · Day 2 — The first rep
**Subject:** Your first rep is smaller than you think.
**Preview:** Leaving the apartment counts. Really.
**Segmentation:** everyone; suppress if onboarded.

People imagine rebuilding a social life means becoming someone who "goes out." It doesn't. It means one rep: leave the apartment on purpose, be somewhere public for 45 minutes, come home.

That's it. The confidence people think they need first? It shows up mid-rep, not before. Same as the gym.

**One action today:** one solo-but-public outing. A cafe with your book counts completely.

## Email 3 · Day 4 — The follow-up gap
**Subject:** The person you met last week won't follow up for you.
**Preview:** Weak ties have a half-life. Here's the window.
**Segmentation:** everyone.

Here's where almost everyone loses the thread: you meet someone good, both say "we should hang out," and both wait. The tie dies of symmetry — nobody was rejecting anyone.

The fix is one low-pressure message inside 72 hours: "Good meeting you at [place]. I'm going again next week — you in?" Timing beats wording, every time.

**One action today:** think of the last person you clicked with. Send the five-word version. No apology for the delay.

→ [Orbit drafts these — you hit send]

## Email 4 · Day 7 — Become a regular
**Subject:** Stop chasing novelty. Become a regular somewhere.
**Preview:** The third visit is the threshold.
**Segmentation:** everyone.

You could attend a different event every week for a year and still not have a friend — every new room starts you at stranger-zero. Repetition is the compounding asset: visit one you're a stranger, visit two you're familiar, visit three people expect you. Expected people get invited to things.

**One action today:** pick the one recurring thing you'd survive attending three times. Put the next occurrence in your calendar.

→ [Score your events — recurring wins on purpose]

## Email 5 · Day 10 — The drained night
**Subject:** For the nights you want out but have nothing left.
**Preview:** The minimum viable outing exists for a reason.
**Segmentation:** everyone; especially resonates with introvert-segment if you tag signups by source.

Drained-but-restless is the most common Friday feeling nobody plans for. The all-or-nothing frame (Whole Social Night vs. couch) is what keeps you home.

Orbit's answer is the minimum viable outing: low effort, public, complete. Reading at a cafe counts. A walk that ends somewhere public counts. These nights keep the leaving-the-apartment muscle warm so the motivated nights have something to build on.

**One action tonight:** the minimum version. Nothing more.

## Email 6 · Day 14 — The 30-day rebuild
**Subject:** Thirty days, one ordinary rep at a time.
**Preview:** Week 1 is just leaving the apartment.
**Segmentation:** everyone.

The full program, since you've now seen the pieces: Week 1, leave the apartment. Week 2, start conversations. Week 3, follow up. Week 4, build cadence — pick your anchors and become a regular.

One assignment a day. And the only rule that matters: miss a day → continue. Restarting is a trap disguised as discipline.

**One action today:** start day 1. It's tonight-sized.

## Email 7 · Day 18 — Honest close
**Subject:** Where Orbit fits (and where it doesn't)
**Preview:** No feed, no matching, no magic. Just the loop.
**Segmentation:** everyone who hasn't activated; suppress for active users.

Straight answer on what this is: Orbit is a private system for the go-out → follow-up → repeat loop. It plans your nights, scores your events, tracks who you met, and drafts follow-ups you send yourself.

It is not a dating app, a friend-matcher, a feed, or an AI companion — and it won't promise you friends, because nobody honestly can. What it promises: you'll leave the apartment, talk to one person, and follow up. Which is what actually works.

If that's the tool you wanted, it's free and takes two minutes to start. If not, no hard feelings — unsubscribe below and no more emails.
